Review what apps and AI agents can access in your accounts

RosmarOps reads what the AI agents on your computer can reach. Agents that run in the cloud, and the apps you connected with "Sign in with Google" or similar, keep their access in each provider's account, where RosmarOps cannot see it. Each provider has a page that lists that access. This one takes you there and tells you what to look for.

What to look for

AI agents and assistants you no longer use. Access to your email, files, calendar or code that an app does not need. Apps you don't recognise. Apps never used, or not used for months: Microsoft and GitHub show when each one was last used. If in doubt, remove the access: you can grant it again the next time you use the app.

Google

Open your linked apps. Look at the apps that have access to your Gmail, Drive or Calendar, not only the ones you sign in with. Each app's details show when you granted access.

Microsoft

For a personal account, open apps and services you've given access: each app shows when you last used it and, inside, every permission it has. For a work or school account, open My Apps; permissions granted by your administrator can only be removed by them.

GitHub

Open Settings › Applications. Review the three tabs: installed GitHub Apps, authorized GitHub Apps and authorized OAuth apps. OAuth apps show when they were last used.

Slack

Open Manage apps for your workspace. If your workspace requires approval for apps, only its owners and app managers can remove them.

Notion

Open your connections. Each connection can be limited to the pages you share with it.

Atlassian

Open connected apps in your Atlassian account. Apps installed on a Jira or Confluence site are managed by the site's administrators.

Dropbox

Open connected apps. Each one shows whether it can reach all your Dropbox or only its own folder.

RosmarOps does not connect to these accounts. Links checked on 30 October 2026.

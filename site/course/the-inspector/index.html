Step 2 of 6 · The idea, with an example

The inspector

Before changing anything, someone has to look.

Imagine that, before buying a flat, you call in an inspector. They go in, open the doors, check which keys there are and who has a copy. They write it all down in a report. They don't fix anything: they tell you what they saw and what they would change. They don't move in either, so they don't see what happens in the flat once they've left. RosmarOps does that job with the AI agents on your computer.

AI agent

What is it?

An artificial intelligence program that doesn't just answer: it also does things for you, like reading files, running commands or using your accounts. Claude Code, Cursor and Codex are agents.

Without asking

What does it do on its own?

Some actions it takes without you having to approve them first. The more it does that way, the further it can go if something tricks it.

Who decides

Who gives permission?

When an action needs permission, one of three gives it: you, another AI model that reviews the action, or nobody. It depends on the mode, the agent's setting that decides how much it asks you.

RosmarOps

What does the inspector do?

It reads the settings each agent keeps in files on your computer and writes you a report: what each one can do without asking you and what is worth changing first.

What it looks at

  • The settings of twelve agents, among them Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and GitHub Copilot.
  • Your projects, which it finds on its own, and the instructions your agents read in them.
  • The keys saved in files on your computer. For each one it notes what type it is and where it is, never the value.

What it doesn't look at

  • What an agent does while it works. It reads what is written in the files, what is called configuration at rest: the settings as they are saved, not what the agent does with them.
  • What an MCP tool does inside. An MCP tool is a piece that adds tools to an agent.
  • The Windows Credential Manager.
  • And what it couldn't read, it doesn't count as clean: it tells you it couldn't read it.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't change your files or your agents. It tells you what to change and you decide.
  • What it reads from your computer doesn't leave without your permission, and your keys never do.

Step 3 of 6 · Try it

Who decides?

The same agent, in three modes. See who gives permission for each action.

Do this, in order:

  1. Look at the selector at the top: “Manual” is selected.
  2. Read the four actions. Only the first one says “Without asking you”.
  3. Press “Auto”.
  4. Look at actions 3 and 4: now they say “Another AI model decides”.
  5. Look at action 2: editing a file no longer asks you.
  6. Press “Bypass permissions”.
  7. Look at the four actions: they all say “Without asking you”.
  8. Read the message under the selector.
  9. Press “Manual” again.

In Manual mode, you give almost every permission. It reads the project's files without asking you, and also runs without asking you some commands that only read, like ls or cat. Everything else it asks you.

Since version 2.1.283, Claude Code starts in auto mode when you open it in a terminal or in VS Code, if that mode is available. If it isn't, it starts in Manual mode.

The example is Claude Code. The facts come from its official documentation, checked on 26 November 2026.

What you've just seen

  • The same agent can do a lot or a little without asking you.
  • It doesn't depend only on the agent: it depends on its mode and its settings.
  • Those settings are written in files on your computer. That's what RosmarOps reads.

Step 5 of 6 · On your computer

Ten minutes. Run RosmarOps for the first time and read the “Result” block.

  1. Download RosmarOps.
    Download it

    What you see: the download page, with the program for your system: Windows, macOS or Linux.

    If you don't want to install it yet, do these steps with the sample report.

  2. Open it. On Windows, double-click rosmarops.exe. On macOS and Linux, follow the instructions on the download page.

    What you see: the program finds your projects on its own. If it finds none, it asks you to choose the folder where you keep them.

  3. Wait for it to finish. It usually takes under 5 minutes; with many projects, up to 20.

    What you see: the report opens on its own in your browser. At the top it says “RosmarOps report”.

  4. Look at the first block, “Result”, and read the first sentence.

    What you see: one of these three sentences:

    • “At least one of your agents can do a lot on this computer without asking you.”
    • “Your agents ask you before most things, but some gaps are open.”
    • “In what RosmarOps checked, your agents ask you before they act.”
  5. Look at the number next to it.

    What you see: a score out of 100 and one word: “Exposed”, “Open gaps” or “Contained”. The higher the score, the less exposed your agents are.

  6. Read the sentence underneath.

    What you see: something like “Score of Claude Code, your agent with the least control: does quite a lot without asking you (class C). With factory settings it would get 70.”

    The agent it names is the one that does most without asking you. Write it down.

  7. Look for the “Start here” card, in the same block.

    What you see: the first thing worth changing. You can do it now, or wait for lesson 2, which explains why it's that and not something else.

What if you don't see a score?

If it says “No score:”, the report tells you why. For example: “no AI agents found” or “some sources could not be read”.

Step 6 of 6 · What you take away

  • An AI agent does things on your computer, and some of them without asking you.
  • Who gives permission depends on the mode: you, another AI model or nobody.
  • RosmarOps reads what each agent is allowed to do, not what it does while it works, and changes nothing.

When another program launches your AI agents

Seven questions to know what they can do without asking you.

If you use an AI agent, you know how it goes.

You ask for something, it asks you before the important steps, and you decide.

You launch it: its questions reach you.

The next step is not launching it yourself.

Orchestrators · They coordinate several agents as a team.

Workflow automation · It chains steps and puts agents inside.

No-code builders · You put an agent together without programming.

They start them at a set time, or when an email arrives.

And keep going when you are not looking.

Another program launches it: it decides which settings it starts with and which keys it gets.

What the agent can do without asking is no longer decided in the agent.

It is decided in the product that launches it.

That is why we ask each product seven questions.

Yes or no, with its documentation in front of us.

1 · Out of the box · Does it say what an agent can do without asking you, as it comes?

2 · The agents' questions · If it launches agents made by others, do they keep their questions?

3 · What each agent inherits · Does it say which folder and which keys each agent receives?

4 · The keys · Can you remove the keys one by one?

5 · With nobody watching · Can you see what starts on its own, and turn it off?

6 · Stopping · Can you stop one agent, and all of them at once?

7 · Security advisories · Does it say where it publishes its security advisories?

We do not measure whether the product is good.

We measure whether you can know what it does.

Before publishing, we tell each vendor and give them fourteen days.

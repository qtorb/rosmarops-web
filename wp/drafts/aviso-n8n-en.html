What happens when you tell a vendor, in public and with fourteen days' notice, what you are going to publish about its product.

On 26 November we opened a notice in n8n's documentation repository. It said three things: that we had measured n8n against the RosmarOps Control Baseline, what we had found on each criterion and with which source, and that we would publish it on 10 December.

It also said what could change it: a public source answering what we had not been able to answer.

What happened

That same day someone commented on the notice. They said the documentation for n8n's agents did not explain two things, the same two we had not found, and that they were going to check.

The next day they came back with the work done. They had gone to the code of the exact version we measured, checked the defaults and brought two sentences drafted for the documentation, with the page each one would go on. And they pointed to a third correction already under way in another proposed change.

They asked us to confirm the wording before submitting it.

What we answered

That we would not. It is not ours to approve n8n's documentation; that belongs to those who maintain it. If we did, we would stop being the one who measures from outside.

What we did tell them is how we will read it: when those sentences are in the official documentation, we measure those criteria again and cite the page. And we asked two questions, so that what gets written matches what the code does.

As of today we do not know whether the person who wrote works at n8n or contributes from outside. We have asked. For the result it changes nothing: a comment is not evidence; a published page or the code is.

Why we are telling this

Because it is what a notice is for. The notice is neither a courtesy nor a threat. It is the part of the method that turns a “we do not know” into a page of documentation that did not exist before, and that benefits anyone who uses the product, whoever does the measuring.

And because it shows the rule we care about most: what we are told in private does not count. Only what anyone can go and read counts.

The notice is public and can be followed: https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n-docs/issues/5816

The result for n8n, with whatever has changed by then, comes out on 10 December.
